# Cerebral lobe clinical teaching draft

Eight existing left and right frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobe
selections share four short clinical correlations in native CT, MRI and the
existing 3D Atlas. They appear inside the folded clinical teaching panel, with
separate modality cautions, unscored reflections and primary reference links.
No extra learner controls, anatomy assets or scan processing are introduced.

## Review the draft

In the authorized Clinical Review area, open Head learning and choose
Cerebral-lobe clinical correlations in the structure and evidence picker.
Each note links to its exact existing CT, MRI and 3D target. To record corrections,
choose Cerebral-lobe clinical corrections in the same picker, or open Private
corrections & saved history inside the clinical section. The form is offered
only when both the clinical packet and imported head revision verify.

Save a draft or correction request, then compare saved history before starting
another version. Notes remain private to the signed-in account and institution,
separate from the anatomy, brainstem, ventricular, cerebellar and deep-grey
histories. Concurrent saves cannot overwrite an earlier version. Changed packet
or session details lock saving; preserve your edits and reload rather than
entering them against another packet. Do not include patient data.

Download working copy preserves the current form and exact revisions locally,
including unfinished edits. Confirm that the file saved and keep it private.
The download does not confirm a server save, import itself or grant approval.
The form records corrections only: it does not modify teaching, masks or source
bindings and never records clinical sign-off or publication permission.

## Clinical validation

The radiologist must verify the source findings, clinical usefulness, wording,
laterality and displayed targets. Retain the limits of two historical specimens,
selected right-hemisphere stroke subgroups and a retrospective visual-field
series. The latter counted 904 field defects in 852 patients, not 904 patients.
The two temporal/parietal discussions concern different study subgroups and
subregions; their whole-lobe masks are not substitutes for those subregions.

The same discussion beside paired structures does not establish bilateral
clinical equivalence. These masks do not define lesions, arterial territories,
language dominance, neglect networks or retinotopic fields. The displayed
subjects are separate: links compare anatomy semantically, not in registered
patient space. Neither automated checks nor earlier packet decisions confer
clinical or publication approval.
