# Pituitary and corpus callosum clinical teaching draft

The existing MRI pituitary and corpus callosum selections now have short clinical
correlations inside Learn this anatomy. Their linked targets in the existing 3D
Atlas share the clinical context and unscored reflection, not the native MRI
observation instructions. Both panels stay folded until opened.

The pituitary note separates anatomical appearance from hormonal assessment.
The callosal note illustrates the limited specificity of splenial diffusion
restriction. Neither attributes a diagnosis to the teaching subject.

## Review the draft

Open Clinical Review, then Head learning. Choose MRI-only clinical correlations
or either individual clinical note in the structure and evidence picker. Each
note includes its MRI target, existing 3D target, imaging caveat and cited
evidence. There is no exact CT gland or whole-callosum counterpart in this mapping;
the CT pituitary fossa and callosal subpart locators are not substitutes.

This extension is read-only. Existing anatomy, MRI-only teaching and other
clinical correction forms do not save or approve it. Keep any proposed changes
with the concept ID and exact clinical packet revision; do not put patient data
in review notes. Opening the section records no clinical sign-off.

## Clinical validation

Check the wording, evidence strength and both displayed targets before sign-off.
The pituitary reference is the Society's official congress abstract summarizing
its consensus, not the complete guideline. The note gives no testing thresholds,
surveillance schedule or treatment recommendation.

The splenial study is a small selected retrospective comparison, not a universal
diagnostic rule. Verify the distinction between DWI hyperintensity, ADC findings
and etiology. No DWI acquisition is inferred from an anatomical image or reformat.

Retain the actual mask limits: the whole-gland label does not delineate separate
lobes, stalk or hormone-producing cells; the saved callosal mask is partial and
is not a splenium, lesion or fibre-tract segmentation. The generic model and MRI
are different subjects. Their links compare named anatomy, not registered
patient-space positions. Automated checks do not confer clinical approval.
