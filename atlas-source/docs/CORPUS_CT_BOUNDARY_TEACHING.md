# Corpus spongiosum: CT boundary teaching draft

This original educational note fills only the CT topic for the existing
FMA19617 / FJ3133 bulb-and-shaft representation. It appears through the shared
Pelvis / Whole body selection; those views are not separate anatomical models.
The included surface is not a complete organ, a CT segmentation or a urethral
lumen. Existing MRI, ultrasound, X-ray and other teaching remain independent.

## Evidence and teaching scope

- [Berná-Mestre and colleagues, 2021, original CT urethrography study](https://doi.org/10.3390/app11031006)
  supports the distinction between a contrast-opacified urethral passage and
  surrounding erectile tissue. The prospective cohort comprised 22 men with
  urethral abnormalities, not a normal-anatomy visibility study. Its discussion
  states that surrounding spongiofibrosis thickness was not measured on CT
  because of insufficient attenuation distinction. The note preserves this
  study-specific limitation; it does not infer routine CT sensitivity, diagnostic
  accuracy, normal measurements or treatment recommendations.
- [NCI: corpus spongiosum](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-terms/def/corpus-spongiosum)
  supports the tissue-versus-urethral-passage distinction. The existing teaching
  also links the [NCI SEER anatomy resource](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/reproductive/male/penis.html).
  The dictionary was directly available during this recheck; the SEER page timed
  out. No inaccessible page is represented as newly read.

The anatomical comparison is semantic. No scan, patient-space transform, slice
position, voxel boundary, patency or fibrosis measurement is supplied. Independent
Atlas, imaging-case and lecture access remains unchanged.
