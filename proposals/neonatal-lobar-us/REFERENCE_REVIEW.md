# Neonatal lobar ultrasound: source review

- **Frontal:** Supported as orientation, with careful wording. Anterior-fontanelle coronal and sagittal sweeps are standard; figure 7 places the precentral gyrus anterior to the central sulcus. Figure 10 shows an anterior orbitofrontal coronal plane in which the central sulcus is not evident. Say “may not be visible in a given anterior plane”; comparing planes is sensible, not a guarantee of identification. [Gonçalves and Hwang, abstract and figures 7–10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33026469/); [EFSUMB 2022, p. 1](https://efsumb.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/neonatal_cranial_ultrasound_safety_aspects_update2022_final.pdf).
- **Parietal:** Supported as conditional orientation. Figure 5 depicts the postcentral gyrus and central sulcus anterior to the marginal branch at the vertex, with the probe at the anterior fontanelle. The postcentral gyrus is posterior to the central sulcus; do not imply the marginal branch alone establishes all boundaries. [Gonçalves and Hwang, figure 5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33026469/).
- **Scope:** Neonatal, open-fontanelle context; visibility varies with maturation, window, technique and operator. Superficial convexity structures can be difficult to depict. Multiple views do not guarantee every boundary. [EFSUMB 2022, pp. 1–3](https://efsumb.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/neonatal_cranial_ultrasound_safety_aspects_update2022_final.pdf). The existing grouped surface is neither a neonatal segmentation nor a registered ultrasound image. This editorial source review is not radiologist sign-off.
