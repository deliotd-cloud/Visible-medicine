# Mandible panoramic orientation — draft evidence

Checked 29 October 2026. All lesson wording is newly written; no article text, images, tables, patient records or datasets are embedded. These references are reading links, not imported assets. No new dependencies, fonts, meshes or fees are introduced. Existing source-mesh notices and commercial licence obligations are unchanged.

- Tadinada et al. (2015), *The reliability of tablet computers in depicting maxillofacial radiographic landmarks*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4574055/ . The original observer study identifies mandibular condyle, ramus, angle, lower border, coronoid, notch and mental foramen among the panoramic landmarks assessed. Its display comparison does not establish universal landmark visibility or validate the Atlas mesh.
- Jung and Cho (2014), *Radiographic evaluation of the course and visibility of the mandibular canal*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25473634/ . The original 262-patient study compares panoramic and CBCT depiction. Visibility differs by molar region and modality. The draft uses this limited observation without converting the reported percentages into diagnostic rules or acquisition recommendations.
- Muinelo-Lorenzo et al. (2015), *Anatomical characteristics and visibility of mental foramen and accessory mental foramen: Panoramic radiography vs. cone beam CT*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4670251/ . Supports premolar-region orientation, variable location and incomplete panoramic visualization despite presence on CBCT; discusses superimposition. No surgical recommendation from the paper is reproduced.
