## Primary evidence and scope

Main read the public author abstracts directly on 17 September 2026 (publisher,
PubMed, author institution and Europe PMC API where web retrieval was unavailable).
Full paid articles were not purchased or claimed to have been read.

- [McGinley et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15338212/): cadaver MRI and
  direct thickness comparison; supports nonuniform forearm-membrane anatomy, not
  universal clinical injury thresholds.
- [Fester et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1053/jhsu.2002.32961): MRI/US in 19
  deliberately sectioned cadaver arms. Complete central disruption is not evidence
  for equivalent sensitivity in partial, peripheral or chronic patient injury.
- [He et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/os.13654): 15-patient Maisonneuve series;
  supports checking extent rather than assuming every membrane is continuously
  torn. Not a general-population accuracy or injury-mechanism guarantee.
- [Durkee et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14682426/): 3 retrospective
  leg cases support a limited sonographic appearance discussion, not a rule-out
  test or proof of patient ankle stability.
- [Mesgarzadeh et al., 1989](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiology.171.3.2717746): normal
  wrist MRI/anatomic correlation; slight retinacular bowing may be normal.
- [Calleja Cancho et al., 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2554376/): 16 normal
  volunteers, retinacular visibility and angle-dependent nerve echogenicity.
  The abstract says 16 volunteers, not necessarily 16 wrists; no pathology accuracy
  is inferred.
