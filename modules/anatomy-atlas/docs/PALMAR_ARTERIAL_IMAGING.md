# Palmar arterial imaging drafts

18 September 2026. This source-bound teaching increment uses the existing hand
arterial surfaces and existing Imaging tabs. It does not add geometry, controls,
patient images or an acquired-imaging connection. Exact selections, source files,
previous pending lessons and bundle hashes are retained in
`content/palmar-arterial-imaging-pins.json`.

## Teaching scope

MRI teaching covers the supplied superficial/deep palmar arterial arches and
common/proper palmar digital arteries. Different groups address arch configuration,
proximal digital distribution or distal digital depiction; they are not a claim
that routine hand MRI resolves every vessel. CT teaching is deliberately limited
to the paired superficial palmar arterial arch, for which the cited study directly
examined configuration and variation. Other CT slots, ultrasound, X-ray and all
existing non-imaging topics are unchanged. Arterial evidence is not transferred
to venous arches or digital veins.

The current batch contains 22 source selections: four arterial arches, eight
common digital and ten proper digital arteries. This is an asymmetric available
source set, not a complete normal bilateral digital arterial tree. It supplies
24 draft placements (22 MRI and two CT) using five shared group/topic texts.

The source's numbered common digital arteries retain their original names; no
unverified web-space, perfusion territory or component identity is assigned.
Apparent arch continuity, an endpoint contact or an explosion arrangement cannot
establish a joined lumen, patency, adequate collateral flow or a procedural route.

## Primary references and bounded claims

- Kaplanoglu and Beton, *Evaluation of anatomy and variations of superficial
  palmar arch and upper extremity arteries with CT angiography*,
  [Surgical and Radiologic Anatomy 39, 419–426 (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00276-016-1750-6).
  Public publisher abstract: retrospective CTA of 156 limbs in 78 people, including
  superficial-arch configuration and dominance. Supports variation teaching,
  not functional collateral adequacy or validation of this source donor. Full
  text is subscription-only and was neither purchased nor bypassed. PubMed's
  verification interstitial was not bypassed; the publisher abstract was used.
- Salehi Ravesh et al., noncontrast QISS angiography of wrist, hand and digital
  arteries at 1.5 T,
  [PMID 33582146 (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33582146/).
  Primary abstract: tailored QISS in 20 healthy volunteers examined arches and
  small digital vessels; not all scored segments had diagnostic quality. This
  supports specialised MRA potential, not universal visibility, a routine-MRI
  claim or performance in an unstudied disease population. No protocol settings,
  source images, segment map or classification figures are reproduced.
- Kransdorf et al., *Magnetic resonance angiography of the hand and wrist:
  evaluation of patients with severe ischemic disease*,
  [PMID 9524324 (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9524324/).
  Small historical clinical series reports incomplete depiction of digital
  vessels and some in-plane arch segments. It illustrates acquisition-dependent
  limitations, not current diagnostic accuracy or a recommended examination.
- Hashem et al., *Variations in the origins and absence of the common digital
  arteries of the hand: a cadaveric study*,
  [author institutional abstract (2018)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1550741),
  DOI 10.1177/1753193418764289.
  Direct cadaver investigation describes variable common-digital origins and
  relationships to flexor tendons. It does not identify a variant in our donor
  or establish a safe operative plane. Source numbering must not be silently
  equated with a study's particular web-space branch.
