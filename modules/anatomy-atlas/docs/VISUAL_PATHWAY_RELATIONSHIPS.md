# Chiasm, pituitary and MRI orientation

Open **Brain → Dissect brain → Optic chiasm and tracts → Study view → Chiasm & pituitary**. The existing menu selects the chiasm, hides the tracts, resets separation/cutaway and opens from the right. The pituitary is a nonselectable colour-keyed landmark. Rotate freely or turn it off with **Show pituitary landmark**; no extra toolbar is added.

The gland disappears during any separation layout and returns when separation reaches zero. Its bundle loads only when the landmark is shown. Loading failure/retry, fade, stable cutaway bounds and return to standard views reuse the existing controls. Switching selection, changing layers, Undo or Reassemble exits the relationship view. The original four posterior landmarks remain a separate option; the enclosing solid brain stays suppressed.

## MRI teaching and evidence

Two short MRI drafts in **Learn more → MRI** serve the chiasm and paired tracts. CT/US remain pending. Source surfaces, diffusion-derived orientation and patient correspondence are distinguished; none is substituted for another. Primary material read on 10 September 2026:

- [Chiasmal location and adjacent angles on 3D MRI (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30879711/): indexed primary abstract of a retrospective normal-study series. Direct opening returned no text. Supports variation and the relationship prompt, not a universal measurement or surgical prediction rule.
- [Chen et al., coronal sectional anatomy (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19837645/): public abstract comparing cadaveric sections and normal-adult MRI. Supports cross-sectional localisation; it does not validate this source model.
- [Vinogradov et al., high-resolution 3T study (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16028247/): public abstract of a specialised-coil pilot. Used to distinguish anatomical imaging from diffusion-derived orientation, not to claim routine axonal visibility.

All new text is original factual paraphrase with links. No source diagram, figure, scan, article, patient case, lecture or question bank is redistributed or relicensed. No new dependency, font, texture or paid service is used. The guide is educational context, not a diagnostic or acquisition protocol.
