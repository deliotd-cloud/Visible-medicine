# Toe-phalanx ultrasound surface orientation — draft

Twenty-eight existing source selections are proposed for the Ultrasound tab:
bilateral proximal and distal hallux phalanges, and proximal, middle and distal
phalanges of toes two through five. Five original teaching families distinguish
joint ends and the hallux's two-phalangeal pattern. Sided placements are not
independent curricula. The eight pending cuboid/cuneiform selections are outside
this draft. Existing CT, MRI and X-ray toe lessons remain separate.

## Factual reading and rights

- Hung et al. (2020), [Advanced Ankle and Foot Sonoanatomy](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/10/3/160), CC BY 4.0. General foot surface-window context; this review is not diagnostic-accuracy evidence for these meshes.
- Pistoia et al. (2025), [high-resolution ultrasound of toe pulley anatomy](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00256-025-04901-w), CC BY 4.0. Cadaveric and small healthy-volunteer observations distinguish long- and short-axis plantar soft-tissue levels near lesser-toe MTP joints, proximal phalanges, PIP and DIP levels. Fifth-toe landmarks were less consistently visible. This does not validate a pulley mesh part, universal visibility or fracture detection.
- Moroni et al. (2022), [hallucal interphalangeal ossicle anatomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8938480/), CC BY 4.0. Cadaveric sagittal/axial ultrasound relates hallux proximal and distal phalanges, plantar plate, flexor hallucis longus and a variable plantar IP ossicle. Its small cadaver sample and surgical purpose do not establish a universal ossicle, a source-mesh identity or a clinical pathway for this atlas.
- Le Minor et al. (2016), [variation of lateral-toe middle phalanges](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joa.12462), factual reading only; reuse rights not established. Biphalangeal lateral-toe patterns occur, particularly in the fifth toe. These selected source records have middle phalanges, but that is not a universal patient pattern. No article content or media is imported.

The lessons use brief original anatomical interpretation and reading links.
No article wording, figure, sonogram, protocol image, scan, dataset or derivative
visual is imported. The article licence does not clear separately credited
third-party material. Existing BodyParts3D attribution remains; no new mesh,
dependency, font, texture, paid service or mandatory media licence is introduced.

## Source and clinical limits

Only the 28 exact sided whole-bone records should receive these five families.
The adapter and pins must require complete source identity, including component,
frame, bundle and displayed record. FMA, name or laterality alone cannot bind
teaching. The hallux has an IP joint without a middle phalanx; these selected
lesser toes have PIP and DIP levels. Pistoia et al. label one great-toe
soft-tissue level "DIP"; the learner copy retains the anatomical hallux IP term.
A local acoustic window does not show an entire phalanx.
The static mesh contains no acquired echo, measured soft-tissue attachment,
cartilage, marrow, fracture finding, patient coordinate frame or diagnostic
performance. Ultrasound orientation grants no imaging-case or paid-lecture access.

All copy remains a draft pending revision-bound radiologist review of the exact
source records, displayed wording, modality limits and useful acoustic landmarks.
Technical tests, future browser checks and case clearance are separate from
clinical sign-off. No source generation, website integration, publication or
approval is claimed by this draft.
