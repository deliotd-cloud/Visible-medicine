## Evidence and interpretation

- [University upper-limb artery table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/arteries_upperlimb.html): usual origins, naming transitions and branch distinctions. No copyrighted table, drawing or wording is reproduced.
- [Harder et al., 1983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6421686/): historical intravenous DSA depiction of major upper-limb trunks. DSA is a subtraction technique, not necessarily catheter contrast delivery. This does not establish a current protocol, small-branch detection rate, healthy population performance or modern safety claim. No doses or images are imported.
- [Shetty et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36168778/): primary dissection and separate CTA variation observations. Not catheter DSA, plain-film visibility or a pooled branch-detection estimate. No donor-specific variation is inferred.
- [Bagoji et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24551680/): one cadaver with rare axillary branching. Its named deep brachial stem is not automatically the normal profunda brachii or either Atlas side.
- [Adult-elbow arterial study, 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9384425/): injected/dissected cadaveric arcade anatomy. Not living angiographic accuracy, a complete source network or guaranteed collateral filling.
- [Yamaguchi et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10077790/): injected cadaveric ulnar-nerve vascularity, including lack of an identifiable direct superior-ulnar-collateral/posterior-ulnar-recurrent connection in most specimens. Clinical importance was not established; nerve geometry and perfusion are not supplied here.

Routine unenhanced plain radiography does not outline a normal arterial lumen.
Contrast X-ray angiography/DSA, CTA, MRA and ultrasound remain distinct acquisition
contexts. Actual side/projection comes from the examination. Model reassembly
restores reference orientation, not patient-space registration. Atlas, imaging-case
and paid-lecture access remain independently entitled.
