## Teaching distinctions

- C1: ring injury versus associated ligament injury; separation does not test transverse-ligament stability.
- C2: dens, body and posterior-element injury are not one uniform pattern; C1–C2 and C2–C3 relationships stay distinct.
- C3–C7: bone-only assessment cannot exclude cord injury. The limited NICE MRI-after-CT statement is clinical context for suspected cord injury, not a general scan-order rule or an implemented pathway.
- Thoracic vertebrae: low-energy compression injury and other causes of weakened bone; appearance alone does not establish cause or fracture age.
- T11–L1: compression, burst and distraction patterns differ; stability is not inferred from a reference surface.
- L2–L4: stenosis involves bone and soft tissues, with emergency cauda-equina warning context; vertebral labels are not root diagnoses.
- L5: pars defect versus displacement, and isthmic versus degenerative mechanisms; a pars defect does not guarantee a slip.
- Sacrum: insufficiency injury can be missed on radiographs; a fused reference bone is not a marrow or neurological examination.
- First rib: associated neurovascular injury is possible, not inevitable; no angiography algorithm or risk percentage is imported.
- Second rib: painful breathing and secretion clearance; a bone/cartilage landmark is not a procedural entry point.
- Ribs 3–10: radiographic flail segment versus clinical flail chest. The WSES/CWIS definition is identified and variation between study definitions is acknowledged.
- Ribs 11–12: floating attachment pattern is not a fracture. Thoracoabdominal warning signs do not specify an organ injury from rib number alone.
- Manubrium: normal junction/fusion variation versus injury; three selectable sternal parts are not three freely moving bones.
- Sternal body: a fracture alone does not establish cardiac injury, but does not justify bypassing clinical assessment.
- Xiphoid: variable morphology may resemble a mass; a new lump or pain still needs assessment.

## Evidence and rights

References were inspected on 9 September 2026 through direct text and relevant indexed excerpts/abstracts. The runtime definitions contain the precise links:

- [NICE NG41](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng41/chapter/Recommendations): neurological findings and adult trauma imaging context, not copied assessment rules or treatment algorithms.
- AO Surgery Reference C1 and upper-cervical injury pages: short anatomical injury distinctions only. Search also surfaced NC-ND classification posters; those posters, graphics, tables and classifications are not imported.
- AAOS OrthoInfo: thoracolumbar fractures, osteoporotic compression fractures, lumbar stenosis and adult spondylolisthesis. Generic descriptions that might imply a spinal cord at every lumbar level are not transferred to this model; no treatment or prognosis rules are reproduced.
- NHS and Cambridge University Hospitals: back/chest-injury warning and breathing context. Emergency wording is UK-labelled, not a complete triage tool.
- [WSES/CWIS position paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11487890/): flail-segment versus flail-chest terminology only, no fixation indications, technical approaches or evidence tables.
- [EAST guideline](https://www.east.org/education-resources/practice-management-guidelines/details/blunt-cardiac-injury%2C-screening-for): sternal fracture is not itself a cardiac diagnosis. No monitoring/discharge or screening algorithm is imported.
- Sacral insufficiency [imaging study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9456416/) and [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7964142/): detection limitations; no population sensitivity applied to a patient.
- First-rib [historical cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1130843/) and [review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9158123/): association only. Old selected-trauma cohorts are not a current prevalence or prognosis estimate.
- Sternal/xiphoid studies and case report: PubMed 34280594, 24100061, 29098125 and 23839070. Available abstracts support variability, not universal shape, age thresholds or a diagnosis. Full-text systematic review is not claimed.

All new prose is brief original factual synthesis. No publisher prose, scans, diagrams, photographs, article files, question banks or classification tables were imported.
