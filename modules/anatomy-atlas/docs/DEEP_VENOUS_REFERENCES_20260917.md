# Deep lower-limb vein teaching: source/reuse audit

Checked 17 September 2026. Six exact existing sources, three named families,
Clinical and Pathology only. Original Anatomy/Function and imaging are unchanged.
Family identities and model limitations are inherited from the admitted
deep-leg-veins source and its frozen identities, not invented patient anatomy.

## Admitted text context

- [NHLBI: Deep vein thrombosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/deep-vein-thrombosis):
  deep-leg/thigh DVT, possible pulmonary embolism, symptoms, compression
  ultrasound and clinical correlation. The page was opened, including its dated
  September 2022 text; no treatment protocol or current-guideline claim is used.
- [NHLBI reuse policy](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/about/contact/trademark-branding-and-logo):
  most site information is public domain, with third-party exceptions and no
  endorsement. The requested institutional credit appears in each draft. No
  logo, formatted publication, image, animation or video is copied. In particular,
  the DVT page's Nucleus Medical Media animation is explicitly all-rights-reserved
  and excluded. Public-domain text status does not license that animation.
- Akram F, Fan BE, Tan CW, Teoh WC, Prandoni P, Yap ES. [The clinical application
  of venous ultrasound in diagnosis and follow-up of lower extremity deep vein
  thrombosis (DVT): a case-based discussion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12959-023-00550-y).
  Thrombosis Journal 21:110 (2023). Opened the publisher's Background, Case 1/2
  discussion and article-specific Rights and permissions. CC BY 4.0 explicitly
  permits adaptation with credit, changes indicated and licence link. The compact
  original summary concerns acquired ultrasound evidence, uncertainty in dating
  thrombus and chronic post-thrombotic change. Credit, DOI and licence link are
  visible in every draft. No cases, figures, tables, scan protocols, diagnostic
  performance rates, follow-up intervals or anticoagulation advice are imported.

The existing model has no clot, lumen, valves, compression response or flow.
Naming a calf versus thigh source does not establish an entire examination,
variant venous channels, a validated confluence, patient registration or disease.
The clinician must review each revision before release. Linked case/lecture
access remains independent and no private scans were read or redistributed.

## Excluded candidates / boundaries

Lee et al. 2017 (PMC5381851 / doi10.14366/usg.17001), linked by the pre-existing
Anatomy note, was not used as adaptable text or media for this increment. The
publisher PDF search extract identifies CC BY-NC 3.0; direct fetch failed and
PMC showed a browser challenge. A citation is not a commercial asset grant.
No access restriction was bypassed. The 2007 Gaitini article could not be fully
verified through publisher access; a third-party CC BY search result alone was
not accepted. A 2024 MDPI anatomy candidate returned429 and was not admitted.
TTUHSC thigh/leg tables were inspected but do not provide the needed detailed
vein entries; their copyrighted tables/illustrations were not imported or
claimed to be open assets. These attempts do not broaden the admitted sources.

No new model, font, texture, dependency, paid API or mandatory service is added.
Original app code retains its existing terms; model CC BY4.0 notices remain.
This bounded source audit is neither project-wide legal clearance nor clinical
sign-off. The exact changed12/preserved9,906 transition and actual UI/test
evidence are recorded separately in DEEP_VENOUS_CLINICAL.md and the checkpoint.
