# Upper-limb venous clinical teaching

17 September 2026. Twelve new Clinical/Pathology draft placements for six exact
existing selections: medial brachial veins FMA22935/22936, median cubital veins
FMA22964/22965 and median antebrachial veins FMA22968/22969. They remain available
in the existing Shoulder & arm and Forearm panels, with no extra toolbar controls.
Six topic texts distinguish deep and superficial systems, variable cubital
connections, superficial inflammation and deep-vein thrombosis/embolic risk.

All original source geometry, side labels, Anatomy/Function/Quiz/imaging copy,
recipes, access rights and private review records stay unchanged. Each selection
has one original source file and one connected component in the prior source
audit. This is not six complete venous networks or evidence of patent junctions.
Medial brachial sources do not supply companion veins; superficial surfaces do
not demonstrate all possible patterns, perforators, valves or a safe access site.

## References, claim scope and commercial use

Terra Medium collected references read-only; main opened and checked the actual
pages on 17 September 2026 before writing short original notes:

- [UAMS selected upper-limb veins](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/vein-tables/selected-veins-of-the-upper-limb/):
  superficial palm/forearm drainage, median cubital cephalic/basilic relationship,
  variable median antebrachial drainage and basilic/brachial axillary contribution.
  No table, illustrations, logo or wording reproduced.
- [Mikuni, Chiba and Tonosaki, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23131916/),
  Anatomical Science International88:46–57, DOI10.1007/s12565-012-0160-z.
  The primary cadaver study's abstract supports varied superficial connections,
  absent median cubital configurations and varied nearby neurovascular anatomy.
  Full article not retrieved. No figures, prevalence estimates, classification
  scheme or proposed safe puncture zone imported. Its conclusions are not a
  procedural clearance for the Atlas's source model or a patient.
- [ACR/RSNA RadiologyInfo upper-extremity DVT](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/acs-upper-extremity-dvt):
  deep venous clot, possible arm symptoms and need for appropriate evaluation/
  imaging. Outbound citation and original synthesis only; the publisher expressly
  encourages linking rather than copying. No scan or diagnostic protocol included.
- [NHS phlebitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/phlebitis/), page reviewed11March2026:
  possible arm involvement, local inflammatory features, cannula/injection
  association and need to consider other causes including DVT. No photographs
  imported (the page includes separately credited third-party photography).
- [NHS DVT](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/):
  arm symptoms, urgent evaluation and emergency escalation for DVT symptoms with
  chest pain/breathlessness; embolism is a potential complication, not an Atlas
  risk prediction. No treatment regimens or patient-care algorithms reproduced.

## Regression protection

Exact source identities, both source bundles, the12 previous pending lessons and
full baseline at Atlas46fbcf88 are frozen in upper-venous-clinical-pins.json.
Runtime matches complete canonical records, not names or FMA alone. Only Clinical
and Pathology are changed. Returned arrays are detached, changed source/side/
region/files fail closed. Imaging pending text still truthfully states that
teaching has not been validated; new clinical drafts do not confer validation.

Build/renderer fingerprints and browser tests are separate checks; consult the
coordinating checkpoint for executed evidence, not this test specification alone.

## Required radiologist acceptance

Review the deep/superficial distinction, correct source extent and laterality,
variant/absence wording, diagnostic uncertainty and urgency advice. Check whether
the notes are sufficient for the intended learner; these are introductory drafts,
not comprehensive vascular teaching. The source surfaces cannot establish disease,
compression, Doppler flow, a safe needle route or patient correspondence. Actual
CT/MRI/US/X-ray resources and independently entitled lectures require their own
cleared anchors and review. No source review is transferred to these new revisions.
