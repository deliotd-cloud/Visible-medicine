# Right lower limb venous orientation, source-bound v2

The existing whole-body draft tour retains ID `right-lower-limb-venous-orientation`
and now has revision `right-lower-limb-venous-orientation-v2`. It presents nine
right-sided source selections in this order: small saphenous, popliteal,
anterior tibial, posterior tibial, femoral, deep femoral, great saphenous,
external iliac and common iliac. Four faded source bones (right hip bone,
femur, tibia and fibula) provide orientation. This order is a teaching sequence,
not a reconstructed continuous venous route.

The exact nine vessel records, four bone records, seven display bundles,
source version, coordinate system and CC BY 4.0 license are pinned in
`content/lower-venous-tour-v2-pins.json` from Atlas baseline
`c22c42edc7a663be4cce788d882edb0a83b752fd`. The tour admits those
records by complete canonical source identity. Duplicate IDs, FMA aliases,
changed bundle records or a changed tour sequence require renewed review.
No mesh, geometry, source hold or morphology is changed.

The anterior tibial selection retains two source parts, without separate
validation as companion veins. Other calf source groups do not establish all
companions or exact tibial/popliteal unions. The femoral vein is in the deep
system; the deep femoral (profunda femoris) vein is a separate tributary, not
its serial continuation. Selected surfaces do not prove exact junctions,
continuous lumen, complete tree, valves, flow, reflux or patency. Camera framing
compares nearby venous selections; faded bones remain in the shared source
frame. Neither colour, visible contact nor fading establishes an anatomical
connection. No procedural route, acquired scan or patient registration is
provided.

## Reading evidence

- [UAMS selected lower-limb veins](https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/vein-tables/selected-veins-of-the-lower-limb/) supports general superficial/deep drainage orientation.
- [Quinlan et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12821771/) reviewed 808 thrombus-free limb venograms and observed femoral/popliteal pattern variation; this does not validate these mesh junctions.
- [Cross et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10982994/) combined cadaveric fossae and venograms to describe variable deep-calf union near the popliteal region.
- [Bundens et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7563535/) documented the misleading “superficial femoral vein” term for a deep-system vessel.
- [Almgren et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2313828/) described profunda variation in selected phlebography cases; its cohort does not supply a universal junction or normal reflux rule.
