## Hand distinctions

- Scaphoid: occult early fracture, proximal blood-supply vulnerability and nonunion context; not a perfusion or healing test.
- Lunate: Kienböck osteonecrosis is distinct from traumatic lunate dislocation; no single wrist shape establishes the diagnosis.
- Triquetrum, pisiform and central carpals: an isolated fracture and a complex bony/ligamentous injury are different possibilities. Pisiform is distinct from triquetrum and hamate hook; capitate and trapezoid remain separately identified.
- Trapezium: basal thumb joint arthritis and symptoms, without conflating trapezium and trapezoid.
- Hamate: hook versus body and adjacent ulnar nerve context, without a safe neurovascular corridor or branch diagnosis.
- First metacarpal: joint-involving Bennett/Rolando context versus extra-articular injury.
- Second–fourth metacarpals: rotation, length and the entire digit's alignment.
- Fifth metacarpal: a boxer's neck fracture is not every fifth-metacarpal fracture or proof of injury mechanism.
- Thumb proximal phalanx: MCP collateral-ligament tear versus bony avulsion; no middle phalanx or simulated stress test.
- Finger proximal phalanges: joint involvement, rotational deformity and whole-hand function.
- Finger middle phalanges: dorsal central-slip versus palmar volar-plate injury at the PIP joint.
- Finger distal phalanges: terminal extensor injury with or without a bony avulsion; not every fingertip injury is mallet injury.
- Thumb distal phalanx: IP joint, nail bed, pulp and bone remain distinct; no drainage or wound procedure.

## Foot distinctions

- Talus: vascular injury and post-traumatic arthritis are risks, not inevitable outcomes.
- Calcaneus: subtalar, heel and soft-tissue injury; other injuries can accompany a high-energy event.
- Navicular: stress injury may be occult on early radiographs; not the wrist scaphoid or an accessory-navicular model.
- Cuboid: lateral-column length and joint relationships, not simulated surgical distraction.
- Cuneiforms: exact medial/intermediate/lateral identities; Lisfranc injury can be ligamentous with no obvious fracture.
- First metatarsal: hallux rigidus concerns the first MTP joint, not the hallux IP joint.
- Second metatarsal: Freiberg head disease, not every metatarsal pain or stress fracture; no exclusive-bone or fixed-age rule.
- Third/fourth metatarsals: traumatic versus repetitive-load injury and radiographic limitations.
- Fifth metatarsal: tuberosity avulsion, Jones-region injury and proximal-shaft stress injury are not interchangeable.
- Hallux proximal phalanx: first-MTP plantar-complex injury versus fracture.
- Lesser-toe proximal phalanges: MTP plantar-plate instability versus bone injury.
- Lesser-toe middle phalanges: PIP hammertoe context, not a distorted bone or a DIP deformity.
- Toe distal phalanges: crush/stub injury, skin/nail and bone distinctions; big-toe and complicated-injury assessment is not the same as uncomplicated lesser-toe care.

## Sources and commercial reuse

[AAOS OrthoInfo](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hand-fractures/) and [BSSH](https://www.bssh.ac.uk/patients/conditions/1021/volar_plate_injury) support the hand distinctions; [ASSH](https://www.assh.org/handcareprod/condition/kienbocks-disease) supports Kienböck context. A general AAOS page's thumb “DIP” terminology was not transferred: the thumb joint remains IP.
