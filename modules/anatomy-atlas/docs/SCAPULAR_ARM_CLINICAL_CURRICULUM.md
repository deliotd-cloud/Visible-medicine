## Useful distinctions

- Anconeus contusion is reported as an unusual cause of lateral elbow pain; the published case also had other injuries. Normal posterolateral anconeus is not the accessory anconeus epitrochlearis associated with some medial ulnar neuropathies.
- Brachialis injury is distinct from biceps tendon rupture or neurological weakness.
- Musculocutaneous entrapment in coracobrachialis is a described possibility, not a diagnosis inferred from local pain. Coracobrachialis itself does not cross the elbow.
- Teres major is not teres minor or a cuff muscle. Myotendinous, muscle-belly and humeral-attachment injuries require distinction from adjacent latissimus dorsi.
- Levator-related pain and rhomboid palsy require broader neck/scapular assessment. Shared group teaching does not independently localise a nerve lesion.

Rare case reports support possibility only, not prevalence, diagnostic performance, treatment thresholds or a universal prognosis. Notes remain educational drafts, not self-examination instructions. No nerve route, tear, haematoma, painful trigger point, bursa, acquired scan or imaging protocol is rendered or validated.
