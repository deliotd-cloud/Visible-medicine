# Internal iliac ultrasound orientation

Original draft teaching is bound to the retained right and left internal iliac
artery trunks (FMA18809 and FMA18810) in
`content/iliac-arterial-imaging-pins.json`. It is available only in the
ultrasound tab when the complete source identity matches. The existing CT and
MRI lessons and the other iliac artery selections stay under their prior scope.

The learner uses the internal iliac origin at the common iliac division as an
orientation landmark. An acquired Doppler examination may assess flow direction
and patency there when the artery is visible. Bowel gas and difficult access can
limit iliac imaging, so nonvisualisation alone cannot establish occlusion. The
selected source surface represents a trunk, not all distal divisions or branches;
it contains no ultrasound signal or flow measurement.

## References and rights

- [Polish Ultrasound Society practice standard, 2014](https://jultrason.pl/artykul.php?a=258),
  English text on p. 195: the internal iliac origin is an orientation point and
  can be assessed with Doppler; bowel gas limits the examination.
- [Whiteley et al., 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8544154/): an original
  56-patient iliac duplex study notes that overlying bowel gas can obscure the
  vessels. Its preparation instructions and diagnostic figures are not lessons.

Both are reading citations only. No figure, table or prose from the publisher
article or its CC BY-NC-ND copy is imported; the short factual summary is
original. No image, scan, source geometry, dependency or new licence class is
added. Existing BodyParts3D source rights remain unchanged.

The atlas mesh is unregistered to any acquired study and cannot establish
patency, flow or diagnosis. Case, Atlas and paid-lecture access stay independent.
Anatomical terminology and lesson limits await revision-bound radiologist
sign-off; this draft is neither clinical approval nor deployment evidence.
