## Factual sources and editorial decisions

References were checked on 8 September 2026 local and remain attached to each definition:

- [Suboccipital muscles](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK567762/) and [suboccipital nerve](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK556133/) support the posterior group. Notes distinguish the triangle borders, atlas/axis attachments and skull-reaching versus non-skull-reaching muscles.
- [Prevertebral muscles](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/prevertebral-muscles) supports the anterior group, with [StatPearls prevertebral overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560569/) used only for agreed longus-capitis facts. Its internally inconsistent ramus wording and conflated rectus insertions are not reproduced.
- [Splenius capitis](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/splenius-capitis-muscle) and [splenius cervicis](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/splenius-cervicis-muscle) support the separate skull/cervical attachment targets and same-side action.
- [Longissimus](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/longissimus-muscle) supports the capitis/cervicis distinction. Capitis origin wording stays regional because the overview simplifies cervical process terminology; exact sites and levels require adjudication.
- [Semispinalis capitis](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/semispinalis-capitis-muscle) and [semispinalis cervicis](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/semispinalis-cervicis-muscle) support their attachment and action distinctions.
- [Intrinsic back overview](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/intrinsic-back-muscles) supports cervical iliocostalis teaching. A column name is not evidence of a direct iliac attachment for its cervical part.
