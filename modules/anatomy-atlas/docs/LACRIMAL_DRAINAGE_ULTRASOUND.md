## Evidence and teaching boundary

- [Bohman et al., PMID 33156146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33156146/) studied 22 eyes with 40–70 MHz ultrasound and mapped upper drainage including proximal canaliculi. Sac-lumen appearance differed at rest, with irrigation and with obstruction; these observations do not define a fixed healthy appearance.
- [Al-Faky, PMID 21258078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21258078/) assessed 24 normal drainage systems with modified ultrasound biomicroscopy. Medial-canthal positioning and cup contact were obstacles requiring an adapted setup. Its separate disease examples are not generalized to congenital anatomy.
- [80 MHz canalicular observational study, DOI 10.1007/s00417-019-04515-8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00417-019-04515-8) included 40 normal eyes and reported 2 mm tissue penetration. It informs small, superficial canalicular depiction; it does not validate routine visibility of the deeper nasolacrimal duct.
- [Machado et al., PMID 28832742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28832742/) compared direct-contact 10 MHz B-mode sac findings in ten subjects with normal drainage and ten with chronic dacryocystitis. This small retrospective comparison provides sac-specific context, not diagnostic accuracy for this Atlas selection.
