# Lower-limb vessel questions

Fifteen original formative questions replace generated identification-only
notes on 30 existing bilateral vessel selections in thigh, leg and foot. They
cover continuity, branching, venous systems, vessel companions and source scope.
They are not 30 unique questions, diagnostic exercises or clinical approvals.

Each question is bound to the complete recorded identity, including FMA ID,
laterality, source meshes and catalogue fields. The superficial medial plantar
selection is explicitly a branch, not the whole parent artery. Paired calf
veins and variable small-saphenous termination are not inferred from a single
static mesh. Existing topics, geometry and guided tours are preserved.

## Reading sources and rights

- [TTUHSC lower-limb arteries](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/arteries_lowerlimb.html): usual arterial relationships.
- [TTUHSC lower-limb veins](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/veins_lowerlimb.html): great-saphenous drainage.
- [Lower Extremity Venous Anatomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3036282/): deep-system terminology and variable small-saphenous connections.
- [Operative technique and morbidity of superficial femoral vein harvest](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4974293/): deep calf pairing and proximal popliteal continuation; its older terminology is not adopted.
- [Point-of-care ultrasound for deep venous thrombosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8411774/): posterior-tibial foot/calf relationships, not a diagnostic or procedural protocol.
- [Deep femoral vein variation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11764913/): arterial companion and anatomical variation.
- [Medial plantar artery cadaveric study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38718967/): superficial/deep branch distinction; no universal branching pattern asserted.

These are factual reading references, not imported or licensed product assets.
Publisher text, tables, figures, scans and meshes are not copied. Open reading
access or a citation does not authorize commercial reuse of source material.
New authored wording and code use the project MIT licence. No new asset, paid
service or dependency is added. Per-page wording budgets are checked.
