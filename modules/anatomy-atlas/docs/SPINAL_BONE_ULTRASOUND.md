# Thoracic/lumbar bone ultrasound orientation — 3 October 2026

Thirteen original educational drafts cover the existing T2–T11 and L2–L4
whole-vertebra selections. No new bone, tissue or imaging dataset is created.
Each lesson requires the entire recorded catalogue identity, source file/FMA,
bundle and source frame; a name or FMA match alone is insufficient. Immutable
source pins replay baseline `a7ed1304`, after the existing foot MRI additions.

The drafts distinguish superficial cortical echoes and shadows from laminar,
articular-process and transverse-process patterns. Thoracic teaching separates
adjacent ribs from the selected bone and explains restricted acoustic windows.
Lumbar teaching differentiates interlaminar windows from holes in an individual
vertebra. Neither isolated atlas labels nor a prominent surface process verify
patient-specific level numbering, marrow, disc or cord appearances.

## Primary/factual reading references

- Avramescu et al., *Sonoanatomy of the thoracic spine in adult volunteers*,
  Reg Anesth Pain Med (2012), [PMID 22354105](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22354105/),
  DOI 10.1097/AAP.0b013e3182464be2. Its 61-volunteer study compares acquired
  imaging planes; fewer conclusive upper-thoracic views do not certify this mesh.
- Karmakar et al., *Sonoanatomy relevant for ultrasound-guided central neuraxial
  blocks via the paramedian approach in the lumbar region*, BJR (2012),
  [PMID 22010025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22010025/),
  DOI 10.1259/bjr/93508121. Phantom, volunteer and anatomical-slice findings are
  not proof of equivalent patient imaging or atlas registration.
- Chang et al., *Sonographic tracking of trunk nerves: essential for
  ultrasound-guided pain management and research*, J Pain Res (2017),
  [PMC5222601](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5222601/); its bony-landmark
  discussion supports original orientation wording only, not asset reuse.
- Doo et al., *The prevalence and clinical significance of transitional
  vertebrae: a radiologic investigation using whole spine spiral three-dimensional
  computed tomographic images*, Anesth Pain Med (2020),
  [PMC7713870](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7713870/),
  DOI 10.17085/apm.2020.15.1.103. The whole-spine study supports numbering caution,
  not a patient diagnosis or automated level mapping.
