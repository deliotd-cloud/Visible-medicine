## Evidence and its limits

- [Fairclough et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16533314/) supports distal iliotibial anchorage context. Display separation is not a physiological motion model.
- [Sher et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21499978/), DOI10.1007/s00256-011-1168-5, addresses proximal anatomy using pelvic MRI and dissection. The primary indexed abstract was available despite an empty direct page response; that did not make the identifier invalid.
- [Khanna et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30593591/) distinguishes finer distal attachment appearances. The two selected knee groups are not a population-wide normal reference.
- [Otsuka et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32807326/) supplies local ultrasound/elastography context in healthy men and cadavers, not pathological thresholds or a whole-tract acquisition.
- [University ligament table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/joints_lowerlimb.html) distinguishes the long plantar, short plantar and spring structures.
- [Melão et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19696279/) supplies transverse-tarsal MRI/anatomy correlation in cadavers. This is complex-level orientation, not individual validation of the displayed ligament's attachments or routine clinical visibility.

No original in-vivo long-plantar ultrasound validation sufficient for a detailed
lesson was established during this review. Broader ultrasound reviews and
cadaveric morphology alone were not treated as that evidence. No veterinary
imaging findings are transferred to humans.
