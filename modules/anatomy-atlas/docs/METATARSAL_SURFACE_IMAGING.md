## Evidence and limits

- AO Surgery Reference, [metatarsal examination](https://surgeryreference.aofoundation.org/orthopedic-trauma/adult-trauma/metatarsals/further-reading/patient-examination): AP, lateral and oblique views and inclusion of adjacent joints.
- AO Surgery Reference, [midfoot assessment](https://surgeryreference.aofoundation.org/orthopedic-trauma/adult-trauma/midfoot/further-reading/patient-assessment): distinct second/intermediate-cuneiform and fourth/cuboid radiographic alignment landmarks. No numerical diagnostic threshold or stability conclusion is copied.
- Reijnierse and Griffith, [forefoot/midfoot US and MRI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10668940/): accessible superficial anatomy versus internal joint/subcortical limitations. The paper is CC BY-NC-ND; it is a factual reading reference only. No figure, table, article text or image is bundled or adapted.
- Banal et al., [metatarsal stress-injury pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19567620/): 37 patients with normal radiographs, imperfect US detection compared with dedicated MRI. No diagnostic performance figure is generalized to every bone, device or population.
- Yesilaras et al., [fifth-metatarsal acute injury study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24342871/): one radiographic fracture was missed by ultrasound; this supports the stated exclusion limitation, not a stand-alone diagnostic pathway.
