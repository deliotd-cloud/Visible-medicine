# Forearm attachment relationships — 15 September 2026

The existing collapsed Muscle attachment relationships panel now covers every
retained forearm muscle/head selection: 21 concepts, 42 sided selections and 38
existing bones. No new model, tendon, footprint coordinate, toolbar or mode.
Select a muscle in Forearm or Whole body, open its attachment panel, then Show.
The action retains the selected muscle and its bony partners; Left/Right remains
usable. Undo/Redo uses the same dissection history. Camera/system changes are
disclosed separately from removal history.

## Anatomical scope

Origins, insertions, source cautions and reference links reuse the established
forearm curriculum, not a new competing set of lesson texts. The explicit
source-identity map connects those drafts to whole bones. Right and left IDs are
pinned separately; named heads of pronator teres and flexor carpi ulnaris keep
their separate origins and do not become whole-muscle representations.

- Superficialis points to middle phalanges; profundus to distal phalanges.
- Brevis/longus radial wrist extensors retain third/second metacarpal targets.
- Thumb brevis and longus retain proximal/distal phalangeal distinctions.
- Digital extensors reach phalangeal partners through the extensor apparatus,
  explicitly not supplied by this study.
- FCU links distinguish pisiform insertion from ligament continuations to hamate
  and fifth metacarpal. No ligaments or complete shared tendon are created.
- Palmaris longus has no substituted insertion bone. Its aponeurosis/retinacular
  attachment text remains visible, with an empty bony insertion row.
- Interosseous-membrane, ligament and aponeurotic origins remain in the teaching
  text; this bony-partner action does not claim to display all those tissues.

Hand bones stay outside the forearm's existing region membership. The panel
lists them as unavailable locally and offers the established whole-body
continuation, retaining selected source and side. Show never quietly imports
foreign-region anatomy. Whole body supplies the bony context, not complete
fascial/tendinous anatomy or a clinical donor footprint.
