# Limb connective clinical teaching

## Scope — 9 September 2026

Twenty-four original Clinical/Pathology drafts cover 12 existing source selections: bilateral long plantar ligaments, forearm and leg interosseous membranes, calcaneal tendons, wrist flexor retinacula and iliotibial tracts. These six paired groups retain independent source identities, categories, laterality and ordered regional memberships.

The update fills existing panels without adding controls, geometry, tissue layers or dependencies. It does not simulate tears, dynamic stability, pressure, nerve conduction, gait or clinical procedures. Explode is a viewing transformation, not a tissue stress test. Injury examples are introductory, not an exhaustive curriculum or a patient diagnosis.

## Sources and reuse boundaries

Consulted 9 September 2026. Brief original factual synthesis only; citations do not grant rights to reuse publisher illustrations, text, videos or scans.

- [Plantar calcaneocuboid ligament morphology — primary cadaveric study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7792160/): distinguish adjacent ligaments and acknowledge normal shape variation.
- [Acute Essex–Lopresti injury — clinical series and review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6116505/): forearm-wide injury context; no treatment technique reproduced.
- [AAOS syndesmosis educational abstract](https://www.aaos.org/videos/video-detail-page/?id=20295__Videos): publicly readable anatomical/diagnostic context only; no video downloaded or embedded.
- [AAOS Achilles rupture](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/achilles-tendon-rupture-tear/) and [tendinitis](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/achilles-tendinitis/): distinguish tendon pain and disruption, without treatment prescriptions.
- [AAOS carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/): median-nerve context; retinacular selection is not a surgical plane.
- [AAOS iliotibial band syndrome](https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/iliotibial-band-it-band-syndrome/): activity-related clinical context, not an animated friction model.

No publisher assets, protocols or question banks were imported. Original MIT code/text and existing BodyParts3D CC BY 4.0 obligations remain; fonts, meshes, textures, dependency versions and lockfile are unchanged. No paid service is added.
