# Source-bound neck teaching

Six original topic texts add sixteen draft placements without changing geometry
or adding controls. The three existing left longus colli parts receive Clinical,
Pathology, CT and MRI notes. The paired inferior thyroid arteries receive Clinical
and Pathology notes. Other 9,902 topics and dissection recipes remain unchanged.

## References and scope

- [Suh, Eoh and Shin 2018](https://ecios.org/DOIx.php?id=10.4055/cios.2018.10.2.204),
  retrospective ten-case longus-colli series: symptom context, deposits and imaging
  correlation. Publisher full text checked 17 September 2026. No diagnostic
  threshold, treatment, prevalence estimate or measurement table is reproduced.
  The upper-cervical example is not assigned to every muscle part. No model tendon
  footprint, calcification, fluid or patient-image correspondence is inferred.
- [Yalçin 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16455326/), primary cadaver study:
  variable nerve/artery relationship only, without population proportions or an
  operative route. Indexed primary abstract checked. Nerve/gland geometry absent
  from this source addition is not invented.
- [Ruan et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12871-015-0052-6),
  single case report: a possible artery-specific complication, not its frequency
  or management. Publisher/primary abstract checked; no case imaging imported.

The “Longus colli study” in the Clinical prompt names the existing Atlas Study
menu view (longus-colli-left), not an assertion that the publication validates
the three rendered parts. Part-specific source cautions distinguish superior,
vertical and inferior parts; lower-level reported deposits do not validate an
isolated-part lesion. Only left source parts exist; none is mirrored.
