## Primary evidence and limitations

Yang et al., *Calcification detection on upper extremity arteries: a comparison
of ultrasonic and X-ray methods*, PeerJ 2023, DOI10.7717/peerj.15855:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10460149/

The accessible primary article describes40 adults on stable haemodialysis and
bilateral radial/ulnar assessment with posterior-anterior/lateral radiographs
and ultrasound. This supports only conditional wall-calcification orientation,
not normal-lumen visibility, flow, patency or small-branch identification.
Its displayed Table2 and reported sensitivity/specificity use inconsistent
denominators. No diagnostic-performance percentage is reused; resolution of
that discrepancy belongs in radiologist review, not an automated correction.

Heye et al., *Preoperative Mapping for Haemodialysis Access Surgery with CO2
Venography of the Upper Limb*, EJVES2010, DOI10.1016/j.ejvs.2009.11.036:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1078588409006157

Primary publisher-indexed methods/results were readable on13 October2026; the
direct page fetch returned403. The visible primary text explicitly describes
209 venograms/116 selected advanced-CKD patients and review of both cephalic and
basilic veins in forearm/upper arm on digital subtraction. Only those factual
observations inform these drafts. No inaccessible method, figure, procedure,
diameter threshold, diagnostic performance or clinical recommendation is inferred.
The2006 Heye and2008 Hyland papers are not needed for this batch and supply no
unverified nonfilling mechanism or additional visibility claim.

The other16 pending forearm vascular X-ray selections remain unchanged: median
veins and smaller/recurrent branches lack sufficient exact evidence here.
Both middle collateral Ultrasound selections and all prior Ultrasound teaching
also remain unchanged. All47 tours/282 stops and original quizzes are preserved.
