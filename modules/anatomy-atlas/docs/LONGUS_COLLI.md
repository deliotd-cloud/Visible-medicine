## Focus and safeguards

The one study has three regional instances, not three different muscles. Whole body shows all 15 retained selections: three muscle parts, C1–C7, T1–T3, occipital bone and left longus capitis. Head & neck retains its existing context memberships (11 visible selections); Spine retains its own (14). No prior vertebral or muscle membership is changed to satisfy the new recipe.

Changed/missing/duplicate structure records, source hashes, bundle bindings or coordinate systems fail admission. The source-specific handler rejects right-only and exam requests before mutating camera/dissection state. Source-bound links reject stale source; right-only study links are unavailable. Adding a study does not certify a surgical plane or a physiological movement.

Six brief original Anatomy/Function drafts describe standard attachment orientation and group function. They explicitly distinguish teaching attachment levels from verified source footprints; cervical innervation ranges differ across the cited tables and remain for review. CT/MRI/X-ray/US notes for these new parts are pending; no scan, signal, calcification or fluid collection is generated.

## Review batch for the owner/radiologist

The owner is the designated clinical signer, not an automatic approval. Review the exact revisions in the private body-review workspace. Check:

1. Left-sided identity, naming and the distinction from longus capitis.
2. Vertebral relationships and source surface extent, especially small excursions across the reference midline; do not crop them merely to enforce a side label.
3. Individual attachment levels, fibres/slips and whether each part is useful at the intended teaching resolution.
4. Context visibility in all three regions, anterior/posterior/lateral rotation, separation reset, labels and touch controls.
5. Anatomy/Function wording and the missing right-side/fascial/nerve/vascular detail. Approve only the reviewed scope.
6. Any future imaging example or segmentation separately; source coordinates and explode displacements are not patient correspondence.
