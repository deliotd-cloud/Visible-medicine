## Useful teaching distinctions

- Subclavius is a shoulder-girdle muscle despite its legacy head-neck navigation. Neurogenic and vascular thoracic-outlet problems are distinguished from a local muscle complaint.
- Scalene relationships preserve the anterior/middle interval, phrenic relationship, second-rib posterior-scalene attachment and anatomical variation. No injection path or validated plexus is implied.
- Platysma is an existing facial-expression muscle and a neck-trauma depth landmark, not a safe barrier. No wound-probing or treatment instructions.
- Sternocleidomastoid and splenius: ordinary action differs from dystonic or compensatory activity; a fixed posture cannot identify the primary affected muscle.
- Longus capitis is not longus colli. Calcific longus-colli tendinitis is taught as a neighbouring differential, not misassigned muscle pathology.
- Prevertebral versus posterior suboccipital muscles: preserve distinct attachments and anterior/posterior ramus concepts. Selected overview sources contain inconsistent summaries; those are not imported as uniform nerve-supply rules.
- Occipital neuralgia, referred cervical pain and proposed myodural mechanisms remain distinct. An anatomical relationship or imaging finding does not establish headache causation.
- Cervical extensor weakness, cord warning signs and muscle-only pain are not interchangeable diagnoses.
- Grouped interspinal/intertransverse sets retain bilateral scope and unresolved per-level/nerve details. Rib-elevator breves do not admit the withheld longi.

The previous count of 36 pending spine-primary muscle entries was a navigation count, not 36 cervical muscles: three entries concern lumbar interspinales and right/left short rib elevators. These are included here without relabelling them. The other 45 entries are cervical or head-neck-route selections, including the shoulder-girdle subclavius.
