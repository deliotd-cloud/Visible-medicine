# Right upper-limb bone orientation tour

Draft teaching sequence for seven existing right-sided whole-bone surfaces: clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna, scaphoid and first metacarpal. Lunate and trapezium are faded hand context rather than stops. The source catalog supplies the exact identities, FMA IDs, display bundles and original positions. The tour uses Whole body with an explicit shoulder-arm, forearm and hand scope; it does not combine a separate specimen or create new anatomy.

Each 14-second stop has a contextual camera frame. The scapula and humerus receive separate close views; the radius and ulna share a forearm frame; the scaphoid sits with the faded lunate, and the first metacarpal sits with the faded trapezium. These camera windows make small hand bones visible while every source mesh remains assembled. Other bones can extend beyond a focused window. The existing 1.8-second quintic orbit changes views with zero separation; the renderer's reduced-motion preference removes the sweep. Exact frames, source identities and transition settings belong to revision-bound review evidence.

The short captions use factual relationships from the [Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center El Paso upper-limb bone table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/bones_upperlimb.html) and [upper-limb joint table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/joints_upperlimb.html). Their wording and presentation are original. In anatomical position the radius is on the thumb side and the ulna is medial. The distal ulna meets the radius and does not directly contact the carpal bones; the intervening articular disc is unrendered. The sources describe general anatomy, not model alignment or clinical accuracy.

Only selected whole-bone exterior surfaces are covered. Cartilage, articular discs, ligaments, tendons, muscles, nerves and individual selectable landmarks are outside this tour. It offers no simulated motion, validated contact or spacing, diagnostic projection, measurements, acquired scan or patient registration. It remains a draft pending revision-bound radiologist sign-off. Shared registry, display-review pin generation and integration are handled by the main coordination task.
