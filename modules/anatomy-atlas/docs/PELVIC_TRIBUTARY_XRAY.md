# Pelvic tributary contrast X-ray teaching draft

Source baseline: Atlas `af90b4afc38d4f18e0ab1d9ac9ef16277761b355`; `content/pelvic-venous-tour-pins.json` revision `pelvic-venous-tour-20261010-r1`. This draft supplies six family topics for exactly the ten existing tour targets: bilateral iliolumbar, inferior gluteal, obturator and superior gluteal veins; right internal pudendal and right lateral sacral veins. The left internal pudendal and left lateral sacral candidates remain held. The short superior gluteal meshes remain short, and the right-only topics do not imply a mirrored left model.

## Direct evidence and scope

- [Hamid et al., 2007, original cadaver and in-vivo venogram study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17373712/): iliolumbar/inferior-lumbar terminology and variable iliac receiving patterns. The observations do not establish either selected donor mesh's termination or normal visibility on a plain radiograph.
- [Takenaka et al., 2023, original CT-based three-dimensional and surgical-video study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37496569/): superior/inferior gluteal, obturator and internal pudendal venous branching varies in 60 half-pelvises from 30 surgical patients. This is cross-sectional/surgical anatomy evidence, not catheter-venography sensitivity, donor-source registration or a universal branch map. The indexed abstract directly supports these limited statements; the publisher page timed out and the PMC mirror presented a browser challenge during the coordinator's check. No access control was bypassed or uninspected full-text detail used.
- [Gest and Schlesinger, Texas Tech University anatomy vein table](https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/veins_pelvis_perineum.html): internal pudendal venous region, internal iliac drainage and pudendal-canal terminology. The table is an educational anatomy reference, not an acquired venogram or permission to reproduce its content.
- [Senechal et al., 2021, original pelvic contrast-venography cohort](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2021.751178/full): selective internal iliac venography and recorded pudendal, obturator and gluteal tributaries in 327 symptomatic female patients. It supports conditional contrast depiction in that selected clinical setting only; it does not test routine plain pelvic films, healthy veins, this male donor or any diagnostic threshold here.
- [Théron et al., 1977, original lumbar phlebography report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/139676/): selective lateral sacral contrast injection and epidural filling in a historical lumbar-imaging setting. Its narrow abstract does not establish the full lateral sacral course, a normal pelvic radiographic appearance, a safe route or acceptance of this right mesh.

The teaching text is original paraphrase of limited factual findings. No article text, figure, scan, measurement set or patient asset is imported. Links are references, not licences or evidence that source figures may be redistributed. Atlas source-mesh licensing and holds remain separately governed by the existing source records.

## Editorial and clinical limits

The six topics distinguish an acquired contrast X-ray venogram from a routine non-contrast pelvic radiograph. Projection overlap and model proximity cannot establish a connected lumen, complete junction or network, valves, flow, patency, procedural route, diagnostic criterion or patient-space registration. The ten source surfaces are selections, not a complete pelvic venous system. These are unsigned educational drafts; revision-bound radiologist review remains required before clinical acceptance. The helper binds exact source identities and the shared access/limitation note without changing the held surfaces.
