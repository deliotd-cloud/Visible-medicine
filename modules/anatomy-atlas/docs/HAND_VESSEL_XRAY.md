This authoring batch covers exactly 36 currently pending root selections in the
BodyParts3D 4.0 Atlas source at `8d32653eaac8b86521618c824214dd6af8e35e29`:
22 arterial and 14 venous selections, grouped into 19 source-labelled families.
The exact selection and component identities belong to
`content/hand-vessel-xray-pins.json`; `content/hand-vessel-xray.ts` supplies original
teaching facts for those families. Existing superficial/deep arterial arch and
princeps pollicis X-ray lessons remain separate.

## What “X-ray” means here

The normal fine hand vessel lumen is not mapped by an ordinary unenhanced hand
radiograph. The arterial projection study [PMID 614070] injected radiopaque
material into fresh cadaver hands and compared dissection with several X-ray
projections. It supplies a lesson about overlap, not patient plain-film
visibility. The contrast-detection series [PMID 10520330] retrospectively
examined selected upper-extremity angiograms; its abstract reports differences
from anatomical and sonographic arch observations but cannot establish why an
individual segment was unseen or unfilled. [PMID 29127118] describes palmar
patterns in patients selected for transradial cardiac catheterization; its
cohort and outcomes do not validate this source mesh or a universal pattern.
[PMID 36068025 / PMC9747343] describes variation in thumb digital artery
origins and nomenclature. That evidence warrants retaining the source’s
first–fourth common-digital labels without inventing a web-space assignment.

Venous references have their own methods. [PMID 27845962] used contrast
venography in nine fresh cadavers, with soft X-ray investigation of palmar
cutaneous tissue. [PMID 4020057] examined digital veins in nine cadaver hands
using sequential angiography, microdissection and other specimen methods.
[PMID 1780719] investigated deep palmar veins after vascular injection and
dissection of 12 fresh cadaver specimens. [PMID 36051780] mapped hand and
digit veins after coloured latex injection and dissection of ten hands from
five cadavers. These are anatomical or specimen-imaging studies, not proof of
routine plain-film visibility, a complete venous tree, or patient-specific
drainage. Arterial papers are not used to validate veins.

## Source-label and review boundaries

- Reset separation to zero before comparing reference relationships. This
  restores the source arrangement; it does not register a patient radiograph,
  angiogram or venogram to the model.
- The radialis indicis has two retained components under one identity. The
  grouped palmar metacarpal vein has three components under one identity.
  Each named palmar digital vein has two unnamed components; these are not
  asserted radial and ulnar branches.
- Common digital first–fourth ordinals are unvalidated source numbering. The
  proper arterial side inventory is incomplete. Digit, medial, lateral and
  laterality words are retained anatomical source labels, not screen direction
  or validated correspondence to an individual patient.
- Deep and superficial venous arch names remain distinct labels. The source
  does not validate complete arch continuity, relative depth, drainage or
  filling. Little-finger vein groups remain held and absent from this batch.
- No atlas selection establishes a filled or patent lumen, disease, the cause
  of a nonvisualised vessel, flow, collateral adequacy, surgical suitability
  or an acquisition/treatment recommendation.
