# Shoulder arterial MR angiography teaching

Six existing selections receive three paired MRI-topic drafts: posterior
circumflex humeral, circumflex scapular and suprascapular arteries. Exact source
identity, ordered component files, region memberships and both bundle hashes are
pinned. No surface, source file, study recipe or other teaching topic changes.

## Evidence and reuse

- [Mochizuki et al., 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8079857/): primary small
  position-dependent MRA study; supports the posterior-humeral caution. It is not
  a diagnostic protocol or a blanket claim that modern angiography has no use.
- [Pan et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39568746/): primary six-case
  series; its upper-back depiction and origin examples support the other two
  texts. These are bounded observations, not accuracy or prevalence estimates.

Only short original factual summaries and citation links are added. No publisher
images, tables, abstracts, scans or videos are copied; no media reuse licence is
assumed. Existing BodyParts3D attribution remains. No new model, dependency,
font, texture, service or mandatory fee is introduced. Automated prose limits
count bilateral repetition per reference conservatively.

## Representation and clinical gates

The two posterior-humeral source components remain one selection on each side.
Suprascapular shoulder/thorax memberships describe one artery, not two vessels.
The mesh proves neither a continuous lumen nor flow, patency, dynamic compression,
collateral adequacy or patient-specific origin. Routine MRI and specialised MRA
are distinguished. Unsupported CT/ultrasound topics stay unchanged.

The owner radiologist must review these drafts against their exact revision.
Atlas, imaging-case and paid-lecture access remain independent. No real scan
connection, procedural clearance or clinical approval follows from this change.
