# Orbital support MRI teaching draft

This draft covers twelve retained BodyParts3D identities, paired left and right: trochlea (FMA49067/49068), common tendinous ring (FMA49072/49073), lateral rectus check ligament (FMA49144/49145), medial rectus check ligament (FMA49147/49148), superior tarsal plate (FMA59091/59092), and inferior tarsal plate (FMA59089/59090). The MRI tab alone is in scope. Exact source identities must match the pins; the static model is not a patient scan or registration.

## Evidence and editorial boundaries

- [Dobbs et al., AJNR 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32241775/) demonstrate the superior oblique tendon course through the trochlea with microscopy-coil MRI and describe the spatial-resolution gain over conventional orbital MRI. This supports sequence-specific depiction, not a routine-scan visibility promise.
- [Ferreira et al., Cancers 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32178233/) identified upper and lower tarsal plates in an eyelid MRI study of 38 normal eyelids. Their comparison shows inferior tarsal plate identification is much harder with a standard orbit protocol than with a dedicated eyelid protocol. This is an imaging-method observation, not diagnostic sensitivity.
- [Ruskell et al., Journal of Anatomy 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15733302/) describe, by human histology, medial and lateral rectus sleeve connections to periosteum. That establishes anatomic context for check ligaments. The lesson makes no claim that either ligament can be separately resolved on routine MRI.
- [Topographical cadaver study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8776787/) found a common tendinous origin at the orbital apex in 20 orbits. [Another histological study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31675425/) reported an independent superior rectus origin and a C-shaped shared origin for three recti. The teaching therefore uses the apical region as orientation while leaving the precise boundary unsettled.
- [Demer et al., prospective extraocular muscle imaging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12506273/) used surface-coil MRI and fixation control to assess muscle and trochlear abnormalities. These purpose-built acquisitions do not demonstrate individual check-ligament or common-ring boundary visibility on routine scans.
- [An orbital MRI investigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6695076/) observed that thick sections and partial-volume effects reduced resolution for small low-signal structures. Its 0.3 T technique is historic, so the draft uses this only as a mechanism for acquisition-dependent limits, not as current MRI performance data.

The authored text is original and imports no source figures, scans, contours or tables. Reduced resolution and partial-volume averaging are explicit limits for small adjacent fascial structures. A missing structure on a slice cannot establish its anatomic absence. There is no acquired scan, patient-to-model registration, clinical performance claim or symptom-based exclusion.
