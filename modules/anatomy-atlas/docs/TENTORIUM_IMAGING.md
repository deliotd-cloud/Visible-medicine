# Tentorium CT/MRI teaching draft

This source-bound addition prepares CT and MRI lessons for the single displayed BodyParts3D FMA83966 tentorium selection. Its identity is the incomplete right-sided source surface in `tentorium-partial`, not a bilateral tentorium, complete notch or patient segmentation. Anatomy and function drafts, all other topics, display geometry, separation recipes and shoulder material retain their prior values. The CT/MRI lessons stay **drafts** until revision-bound radiologist review; no clinical approval is recorded.

The CT note uses the anatomical relation of the dural shelf to posterior cerebrum and cerebellum and teaches comparison across adjacent planes because band appearance varies with slice level and angle. It does not label a single dense band as haemorrhage or pathologic thickening. The MRI note describes T1 postcontrast orientation and the need to inspect the acquired sequence, plane and adjacent images. Normal meningeal enhancement can appear sequence-dependent, so continuous enhancement alone is not a diagnosis. Neither lesson supplies a sinus lumen, patient finding or clinical rule. No acquired scans, intensities, masks, registration or CT-head clinical material enter this change.

## Source and reuse

- Anatomy: Rai R, Iwanaga J, Shokouhi G, Oskouian RJ, Tubbs RS, [*The Tentorium Cerebelli: A Comprehensive Review Including Its Anatomy, Embryology, and Surgical Techniques*](https://www.cureus.com/articles/13852-the-tentorium-cerebelli-a-comprehensive-review-including-its-anatomy-embryology-and-surgical-techniques.pdf), Cureus 2018;10:e3079, DOI 10.7759/cureus.3079. © 2018 Rai et al., [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). The learner text is an original short summary/adaptation with credit and link. No publisher prose, figures or media are copied.
- CT factual basis: Naidich et al., 1977, [PubMed PMID 870939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/870939/), an anatomical comparison of tentorial appearance on axial CT across slice level and angle.
- MRI factual basis: Farn JW and Mirowitz SA, 1994, [PubMed PMID 8273651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8273651/), a historical postcontrast comparison of normal meningeal continuity on conventional 2D spin echo and 3D gradient echo sequences. The study's 69 patients do not establish a current protocol or universal threshold.
- Atlas source geometry and its separate CC BY 4.0 credit, modification details and limitations remain in [TENTORIUM_SOURCE.md](TENTORIUM_SOURCE.md). No research study images, tables, results data or full abstracts are redistributed.
