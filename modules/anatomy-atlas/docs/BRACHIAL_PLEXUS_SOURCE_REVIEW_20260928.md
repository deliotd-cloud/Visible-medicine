# Brachial plexus: source decision, 28 September 2026

Status: **no model admitted or downloaded**. This audit advances the peripheral-
nerve gap in [the acceptance audit](REQUIREMENT_AUDIT.md); it does not resolve it.
The current atlas must not imply that the plexus is present. No new dependency,
asset, clinical decision, scan mapping or third-party licence obligation is added.

## New candidate worth pursuing

[Cevallos et al., Cureus 2026, DOI 10.7759/cureus.104662](https://www.cureus.com/articles/464829-a-reliable-low-cost-three-dimensional-3d-printed-brachial-plexus-model-for-enhanced-anatomical-learning)
describes a scanned right cadaveric plexus, excised and pinned before scanning.
Some absent branches were digitally reconstructed; the axillary artery is absent.
The [publisher PDF](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/technical_report/pdf/464829/20260305-62865-2o7sxz.pdf)
labels the article CC BY 4.0. Its “Interactive Model 1” link resolves to a
[demonstration video](https://vimeo.com/1160945527), not a verified mesh archive.

Decision: promising **acquisition lead**, not a cleared asset. Article rights
alone do not establish a grant for an unidentified downloadable mesh. No exact
file, file-level licence, component inventory or source-space landmarks were
verified. The authors' classroom/research availability statement is not treated
as permission to redistribute editable geometry in a commercial website.

The next external step requires the owner's authorization to contact the authors,
or an author-published archive with an explicit commercial-compatible grant.
No message has been sent. Ask for:

- The original mesh archive, version and explicit licence covering commercial
  web redistribution and adaptation, with required attribution.
- An inventory distinguishing scanned tissue from reconstructed branches and
  identifying individually named roots, trunks, divisions, cords and branches.
- Units, laterality, specimen orientation, any retained landmarks and available
  validation evidence; documentation of removed supports/print modifications.

## Admission and review plan if a usable archive is obtained

1. Save source rights, credit, exact file hashes and component metadata outside
   public delivery. Reject missing/ambiguous grants, fees or incompatible terms.
2. Inspect original geometry without changing identity: finite coordinates,
   normals, topology, laterality, disconnected pieces and printing supports.
   Keep source originals and any cleaned derivative separately hashed.
3. Prepare an **independent-specimen** preview, with reconstructed portions
   disclosed. Do not place the specimen into the existing whole-body frame by
   eye, mirror it into the other side or claim intact axillary relationships.
4. Ask the radiologist to review the labelled branching graph, every individual
   selectable boundary, omissions and relationships from several views. Do not
   split a continuous fused mesh into named branches without boundary evidence.
5. Only then consider a root-body fit using recorded homologous landmarks and
   an explicit reviewed transform. A plausible overlay is not registration.
6. Preserve separate geometry, teaching and imaging sign-off. No acquired-case
   correspondence, nerve-block guidance or exam admission follows from import.

This is a source-acquisition gate, not a request to invent a nerve network or
repeat the same search. Revisit when a file-level grant/archive or source-author
response becomes available. Continue other independently ready Atlas work.
